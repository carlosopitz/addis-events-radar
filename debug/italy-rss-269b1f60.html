]]> Termination of the Validity of the Paper Italian Identity Card for International Travel from 3 August https://ambaddisabeba.esteri.it/en/news/dall_ambasciata/2026/08/termination-of-the-validity-of-the-paper-italian-identity-card-for-international-travel-from-3-august/ Mon, 03 Aug 2026 07:24:09 +0000 https://ambaddisabeba.esteri.it/?p=5754
  • As of 3 August 2026, the paper Italian identity card will no longer be valid for international travel, regardless of the expiry date indicated on the back of the document. The Electronic Identity Card (Carta d’Identità Elettronica – CIE) will be the only identity card valid for international travel.

    • Applications for the Electronic Identity Card (CIE) may be submitted through the Prenot@Mi platform or by sending an email to: consolato.addisabeba@esteri.it.

    For detailed information, please visit the dedicated webpage at the following link:
    https://ambaddisabeba.esteri.it/it/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-italiano/carta-di-identita-elettronica-c-i-e/

    • Please also note that, as of 1 June 2026, Italian citizens residing abroad and registered with AIRE may apply for an Electronic Identity Card (CIE) at any Italian municipality, as provided for by Law No. 11 of 19 January 2026.

    • Please also note that, for Italian citizens 70 years of age or older, Electronic Identity Cards (CIE) issued on or after 30 July 2026 have unlimited validity.

    On June 12, the Italian Embassy in Ethiopia is organizing “Italian World Research Day (GRIM) in Addis Ababa, with particular attention to activities in space”: an initiative involving the Ministry of Universities and Research (MUR) and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAECI).

    ]]>         Completion of the renovation works of the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) of Tikur Anbessa Specialized Hospital (Black Lion) in Addis Ababa https://ambaddisabeba.esteri.it/en/news/dall_ambasciata/2026/04/completion-of-the-renovation-works-of-the-neonatal-intensive-care-unit-nicu-of-tikur-anbessa-specialized-hospital-black-lion-in-addis-ababa/ Thu, 16 Apr 2026 12:00:20 +0000 https://ambaddisabeba.esteri.it/?p=4461 With the inauguration of the renovated Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU) at “Tikur Anbessa” (Black Lion) Specialized Hospital in Addis Ababa, Italy brings a new beginning for newborn care in Ethiopia.

    Implemented by the Italian NGO Doctors with Africa CUAMM, with funding from the Italian Development Cooperation (AICS), the intervention is part of the broader Italian-funded programme “Strengthening Neonatology in Ethiopia”, aimed at improving access to high-quality neonatal care through infrastructure rehabilitation, equipment provision, and capacity building for health professionals.

    In his opening remarks, the Ambassador of Italy to Ethiopia, Sem Fabrizi, who took part in the inauguration – alongside the CEO of the Hospital, the President of Addis Ababa University, the Director of the AICS Office in Addis Ababa, and representatives from the Ethiopian Ministry of Health and CUAMM, emphasised that “Today, we celebrate not only the completion of a renovation, but a renewed commitment to the most vulnerable patients, newborns who depend on timely, safe, and high-quality care to survive and thrive, in line with Italy’s long-standing commitment in the Ethiopian health sector”.

    The intervention included upgrades to the electrical and water systems, renovation of floors and critical infrastructure, and installation of dedicated support systems to ensure the unit can operate safely and continuously. Italy has long been a committed partner of Ethiopia in the health sector, supporting national efforts to strengthen healthcare systems through investments in hospital rehabilitation, maternal and child health, emergency response, medical training, and capacity building across the country. This initiative reflects the long-standing partnership between Italy and Ethiopia in the health sector and our shared commitment to investing in life, dignity, and the future.

    Remembrance is not a formal ritual. It is a collective responsibility towards the future.

    I thank all the Italian Embassies, Consulates, and Cultural Institutes that are reinforcing this message today, all over the world, with many initiatives.

    It is our duty to deliver a world that is free from the virus of anti-Semitism to the new generations.

    As I had the honor of saying when, as Minister of Foreign Affairs, I represented the government at the Yad Vashem Holocaust Memorial Museum on Holocaust Remembrance Day, no one should ever again have to live in fear simply because they are Jewish.

    The abyss of the Shoah struck at the heart of our civilization and left an indelible mark on the history and identity of Europe.

    Remembering is not enough. We must remain vigilant.

    Anti-Semitism has not disappeared.

    It is a specter that continues to haunt our societies, in Europe and around the world.

    Unfortunately, we are reminded of this by cowardly terrorist attacks such as the one at Bondi Beach in Australia, which we condemn strongly and without hesitation.

    But also, by many incidents of daily violence and discrimination, even in our cities, even in Europe.

    Anti-Semitism is an invisible poison. Today, it spreads mainly online and on social media.

    We therefore need the antidote of memory, knowledge, and respect.

    We want to bring it more and more into schools, universities, and our streets.

    The fight against anti-Semitism is, to me, the battle of a lifetime, both politically and personally.

    There is no place for anti-Semitism. It is incompatible with our values, our principles, and our identity.

    The government is at the forefront of the fight against anti-Semitic hatred.

    It is a commitment that I bring to bear every day, both in Europe and at all international meetings, as a sign of our concrete and unceasing efforts to promote dialogue and peace.

    Today, together, we say once again, everywhere in the world: never again!
